Japanese Curry

Why should I make this?

Japanese curry is the culinary equivalent of a warm hug from someone you suspect is holding a knife behind their back—comforting, slightly alarming, and deeply effective. Unlike its spice-forward cousins, this version is thick, velvety, and sweet, designed specifically to mask the crushing realization that you're eating vegetables again. The sauce clings to the potatoes with the same desperate intensity with which you cling to the hope that your weekend won't be spent doing laundry. It's remarkably easy to make, though the bubbling roux will likely develop sentience before your next performance review. Serve it over rice, stare into the middle distance, and remember that even if everything else is failing, at least your dinner is aesthetically pleasing.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon vegetable oil, or more as needed
  • 1 ¾ pounds beef chuck, cut into 2-inch cubes
  • 3 onions, quartered
  • 1 tablespoon ketchup
  • 1 ½ teaspoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 pinch cayenne pepper, or to taste (Optional)
  • water to cover
  • 4 carrots, cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 1 cube chicken bouillon (Optional)
  • 3 medium potatoes, cut into 3-inch chunks
  • 1 ½ (3.5 ounce) containers Japanese curry roux, or more to taste

Steps

  1. Heat oil in a 6-quart pot over medium-high heat. Add beef and saute until brown, 5 to 7 minutes. Add onions and cook until starting to soften, about 3 minutes. Add ketchup and Worcestershire sauce. Stir to coat. Add cayenne pepper. Pour in water to cover mixture by 1 or 2 inches. Add carrots and bouillon.
  2. Simmer, skimming fat off the surface of the broth as needed, for 30 minutes. Add potatoes. Stir in 1 package of curry roux and let dissolve; add remaining curry as needed to achieve desired thickness. Continue simmering until beef and vegetables are tender, about 30 minutes more.
